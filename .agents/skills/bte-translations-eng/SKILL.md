# Baker Tilly Ecuador — Spanish → English Translation Guide

This skill packages the Spanish↔English correspondences confirmed against Baker Tilly Ecuador's own current site (bakertilly.ec — specifically the pages that already exist in both languages: Home, La Firma/The Firm, Equipo/Team, and Internacional/International), plus standard professional English equivalents for Ecuadorian tax, legal, and regulatory terms. Use it whenever translating BTE website copy, service descriptions, team bios, or similar institutional content from Spanish to English, so the result reads as a continuation of what the firm has already published in English rather than a fresh, independent translation.

## Tone and register

BTE's own English pages are formal, third-person, and institutional — measured rather than punchy. Carry this register into any translation, even when the Spanish source is written in a sharper "modern web copy" style (short fragments, rhetorical questions, urgent framing).

- Prefer full sentences over fragment-style headlines. Turn rhetorical-question hooks into plain statements unless a direct question genuinely reads better in English.
- Recurring brand vocabulary to reuse: "certainty," "peace of mind," "trust," "confidence," "track record." The firm's core tagline idea is: "We bring peace of mind to our clients by providing certainty in their decision-making." Reuse this construction (certainty / decision-making / peace of mind) whenever the Spanish source expresses the same "certeza en tiempos de incertidumbre" idea.
- Capitalize key institutional nouns the way BTE's own English copy does: **Partners**, **Clients**, **Staff**, **Community**, **Firm** (e.g. "Genuine relationships with Clients, Staff, and the Community"). This capitalization is used in both the Spanish and English source pages — it's a house style, not a translation error to correct.
- Avoid literal, repetitive triads. Where Spanish repeats one adjective across a list ("grandes relaciones, grandes conversaciones, grandes futuros"), BTE's own translator varies the English adjective rather than repeating "great" three times (see glossary below). Follow that pattern rather than a literal word-for-word repeat.
- Numbers: use English formatting (comma thousands separator — "37,000" not "37.000"; period decimal).
- Keep "Baker Tilly Ecuador" and "Baker Tilly International" as proper nouns, untranslated.

## Notes on source quality

- Most individual service and sector sub-pages on bakertilly.ec exist in Spanish only — there is no official English version to check against for those. The glossary above draws on the pages that ARE bilingual (Home, La Firma/The Firm, Equipo/Team, Internacional/International) plus standard professional usage for Ecuadorian tax/legal terms.
- When a term isn't covered here, follow the tone guidance above and prefer the plainer, more institutional English phrasing over a literal or overly marketing-flavored rendering.
